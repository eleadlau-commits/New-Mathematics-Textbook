# The path to undergraduate algebra

Planning only (11 Nov 2026). Nothing in the book is changed by this file. Each chapter still needs a design agreed by E before it is built.

Covers: groups, rings, fields, Galois, modules, linear algebra proper. **13 chapters** core (2 in Part I, 11 in Part II), plus 2 optional.

## The idea in one paragraph

Every classical "isomorphism / normal form / classification" theorem in algebra is the same shape: a blur of a floor, its kept questions, and its normal form. Uniqueness comes from kept questions (blur's half). Existence comes from Build (words, K[x]/(f)), an algorithm, or **counting bags**. Finite algebra is where the method is strongest, because there existence is often a count, and counting is exact (no estimates, the ceiling found in analysis).

## What is imported

- needs a rule of choice (not in M1–M7): infinite bases (already II.3 R4), algebraic closure of an arbitrary field. ℚ̄ avoids it by Select inside ℂ.

- **Survives / Dies / Appears lines (native):** sign, cycle type, Lagrange (as the bag-size line), all three isomorphism theorems, correspondence, Cayley, abelianisation, orbit–stabiliser, Gauss lemma, uniqueness in Smith / f.g. abelian groups / Jordan / Sylvester, rank, ⊗ universal property, Frobenius, constructibility, Galois correspondence, Kummer.
